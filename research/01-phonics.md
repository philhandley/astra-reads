# Astra Reads: phonics research brief

For Finn (6, Year 2), who is on Little Wandle Letters and Sounds Revised Phase 5 (also Oxford Reading Tree Floppy's Phonics Level 5).
Compiled 11 October 2026. Each section ends with its sources.

## Read first

- Phase 5 is taught across Year 1 (Autumn 1 to Summer 2). Summer 1 is a review with no new GPCs or tricky words. Finn's Year 2 work is consolidation, not new content.
- Oxford Owl matches Phase 5 to Level 5, the Green band. Yellow (Level 3) and Light Blue (Level 4) are the steps before it.
- Little Wandle publishes no words-per-page or sentences-per-page figure. The per-page numbers below are retailer estimates.
- The 2021 programme overview and the January 2025 Year 1 tricky-word leaflet agree on every Phase 5 tricky word.

## 5. Oxford Reading Tree: Floppy's Phonics Level 5 and ORT Stage 5

- Floppy's Phonics: Sounds and Letters is a separate phonics series. It has 42 books across Levels 1 to 5, fully matched to Letters and Sounds Phases 1 to 5. Level 5 has 12 books.
- Level 5 is Phase 5. Books 25 to 36 are all titled "Alternative Spellings". Each spelling is explored through an Oxford Reading Tree scene, followed by spelling activities and short texts.
- Books 25 to 30 are the Level 5 pack. Books 31 to 36 are the "More A" pack. Sellers number them differently, so check the book's inside cover.
- "ORT Stage 5" is the supplier's name for the same books, for example "ORT FP Sounds & Letters Stage 5 Book 25". So Stage 5 and Level 5 mean one set. Do not confuse it with "Read with Oxford Stage 3", a different numbering for home-reading books, which covers Levels 4 and 5.
- Band ladder (Oxford Owl says this is "a guide only"): Level 1 Lilac; 1+ Pink; 2 Red; 3 Yellow; 4 Light Blue; 5 Green; 6 Orange.
- Phase 5 matches Green. Oxford Owl's Level 5 Fiction pack is Green band, aged 5 to 6, and gives "focused practice of phonics at Letters and Sounds Phase 5". Yellow and Light Blue are the earlier steps. One school-made table put Yellow near Phase 3 to 4 and Blue near Phase 4 to 5, but I could not check it against an Oxford source. Some sites call Level 4 "Blue" rather than "Light Blue".
- Words and sentences per page (no official figure found):
- Booklife, Green band: "typically spanning 20 pages with 3 or 4 sentences per page", with longer sentences and more punctuation.
- Bookroo, Oxford Reading Tree Level 5: titles "run around 400 words", an estimate from titles with a Lexile measure.
- Plan for about 3 to 4 sentences and about 20 words a page (400 divided by 20, my arithmetic).

## 6. Little Wandle decodable books and the three reads

- Matching: books are matched to the progression. Year 1 children should be on Phase 5 Set 4 books at most. For extra challenge, use the Stretch and Challenge books rather than moving up a set. The matching grid it links to was not checked.
- Text per page: Little Wandle does not publish words or sentences per page. Word lists for each title can be downloaded after purchase. For a quick check, count the sentences on one sample page.
- Reading practice: three 20-minute sessions a week, each with a different focus.
- Read 1, decoding: apply phonic knowledge to read words, segmenting and blending.
- Read 2, prosody: read aloud with suitable meaning, stress and intonation. Talk about feelings and how punctuation changes meaning.
- Read 3, comprehension: decoding is now automatic, so attention moves to meaning.
- Each session starts with a pre-read of the GPCs, words, tricky words and key vocabulary in the book. This came from a search summary of the same FAQ and was not checked on the page.

## 7. Font advice for beginner readers

- Andika (SIL International, SIL Open Font License) is on Google Fonts at fonts.google.com/specimen/Andika. Its Google Fonts description says it is "designed especially for literacy use, taking into account the needs of beginning readers".
- Single-storey a and g: the SIL design notes say lowercase a and g "look more like handwriting (also known as 'single-story' shapes)".
- Look-alikes: capital I, lowercase l and the numeral 1 are designed not to look alike.
- Weights on Google Fonts: Regular, Italic, Bold, Bold Italic.
- Spacing (Dyslexia Scotland, 12 September 2025): increase letter and word spacing by 30 to 35 per cent of the font size, and use 1.5 line spacing. Younger and less proficient readers may need more spacing than older readers. Spacing can matter more than typeface for some dyslexic readers.
- Serif or sans serif: the same page says "recent evidence shows no consistent advantage of sans serif over serif fonts".
- Single-storey a and g is practitioner advice. Dyslexia Scotland does not mention it, and I found no controlled study of it.
- Other Google Fonts (check glyphs before use):
- Lexend: designed "with dyslexia and struggling readers in mind", wider and more openly spaced. No single-storey statement.
- Atkinson Hyperlegible: designed to increase legibility for low-vision readers and to improve comprehension. No single-storey or beginner-reader statement.
- Comic Neue: a "casual script choice" by Craig Rozynski. Not written for beginners, and no single-storey statement.
- Dyslexia Scotland names Sassoon Primary and OpenDyslexic but says they "aren't necessarily better" than standard fonts. I did not check whether they are on Google Fonts.
- Suggestion: Andika for reading text, with Lexend or Atkinson as options in settings, so Finn can choose.
