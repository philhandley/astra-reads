# 03 Story formula: Explorer Case research

Date: 11 October 2026. For: the Astra Reads storybook for Finn (6) and Zeb (3).
Use: borrow the structure only. Do not copy characters, names, catchphrases or dialogue.

How to read this file
- "Confirmed" means a source checked says it.
- "Suggestion" means my own design advice, not sourced.
- "Summary" means the claim came from a search summary, not a full page read. Check before quoting.
- Sources are listed at the end of each section.

## 1. The Creature Cases (Netflix, April 2022)

Premise
- Animated detective series for about 4 to 7-year-olds. Created by Gabe Pulliam. (confirmed)
- Two agents of the Covert League of Animal Detective Experts (C.L.A.D.E.) solve mysteries with clues and real animal facts. One is the clue-spotter; the other is the animal expert. (confirmed)
- Premiered on Netflix on 12 April 2022. Aired on Nickelodeon from July 2024. (confirmed)

Episode structure
1. Opening: a call or assignment sends the agents to a region such as a desert, forest or jungle. (confirmed)
2. Travel: the agents fly to the location with gadgets. Sources give little more detail. (confirmed, low detail)
3. Clues and suspects: a search for a culprit, with false leads, or for an animal that is hard to catch or wrongly blamed. The animal expert's species knowledge drives the search. (confirmed)
4. Action: a chase or rescue built on a real animal fact. (confirmed, review)
5. Case cracked: the mystery is solved. The closing line or beat is not described. (solved: confirmed; wording: unconfirmed)
6. Fact File: a team of mice restates the facts, with pictures of the real animals and a short dance. (confirmed)

Catchphrases and sign-offs
- Each lead has a signature exclamation or remark. The director sends the agents off with a reminder that the league is counting on them. (confirmed in type)
- Borrow the device (a signature line per character, a send-off line), not the lines.

Art style
- Described as bright and cheery (a viewer comment), smooth, colourful and trendy (Movieguide), with bold animation (press release). (confirmed)
- Animated by TeamTO. (confirmed)

## 2. Octonauts (CBeebies)

Premise
- British-Irish-Canadian animated series for CBeebies, based on the books by Meomi. (confirmed)
- An undersea crew works from a mobile base, the Octopod, and uses vehicles called Gups. Each Gup is based on a real sea animal. (confirmed)
- Each episode features an unusual but real sea creature. The crew must find a vital biological or behavioural fact to rescue the creature or themselves. (confirmed)

Episode formula
1. Problem found: a creature or crew member is in trouble. (confirmed)
2. Octo-Alert: the crew's alarm, sounded when the crew must act. In "The Orca", Captain Barnacles tells Kwazii to sound it. Sources do not say it appears in every episode. (confirmed in one example)
3. Briefing at base: not described in the sources. (unconfirmed)
4. Gup travel: the crew takes a Gup suited to the job. (confirmed; sequence unconfirmed)
5. Meeting the creature in its habitat. (confirmed in general)
6. The real feature solves it: a behaviour, body part or habit of the creature is the answer. (confirmed)
7. Creature Report: a one-minute musical recap. It opens with Captain Barnacles gathering the crew and closes with real photos of the creature (one reviewer). The same song each episode, with the creature and facts swapped. (confirmed; photos from summary)

Catchphrases
- The alarm call is one of Captain Barnacles' catchphrases. (summary)
- A three-word motto is sung at the end of the theme song. (confirmed)

Making facts simple for pre-schoolers
- Facts sit inside the rescue plot, so the child learns the fact because the story needs it. (review opinion)
- The Creature Report repeats the same format every episode, so children hear the pattern again and again. No source tests whether children retain the facts. The repetition effect is my inference.
- Age range varies by reviewer: 2 to 5 in one season review, 3 to 6 in another. (summary)

## 3. Andy's Dinosaur Adventures (2014) and Andy's Prehistoric Adventures (2016), both CBeebies

Dinosaur Adventures: the formula
- Andy Day and a museum co-worker prepare a new exhibition before the museum opens. (confirmed)
- A mishap threatens the opening or the display. (confirmed)
- The grandfather clock starts to chime. Andy knows its secret, runs to it and travels back to the age of the dinosaurs to fetch what the exhibit needs. (confirmed)
- Andy returns to the present using a set return line. (confirmed)
- Twenty episodes, 17 February to 28 March 2014. (confirmed)

Beat by beat
1. Museum prep: build the new exhibit. (confirmed)
2. Mishap: something breaks or goes missing. (confirmed)
3. Clock starts: the chime is the start signal. (confirmed)
4. Countdown: a fan wiki says the clock leaves about 15 minutes after it activates. The official page does not mention a time limit. (summary; unconfirmed)
5. Time travel: Andy goes back and finds the item. (confirmed)
6. Return: Andy gets back to the clock before it leaves. (return confirmed; the time limit is from the fan wiki)
7. Exhibit closes the loop: the page does not say how an episode ends. (unconfirmed)

Prehistoric Adventures
- CBeebies, 2016, 25 episodes, ended. (confirmed)
- Some episodes go to prehistoric times beyond the dinosaurs. (summary)
- Example: Andy looks for a missing prehistoric tumbleweed from the museum and helps a mother dinosaur guard her nest. The episode ends on light humour. (confirmed in TVmaze episode summary)
- The museum name and the clock are described differently across sources. Check before naming them. (conflict)

Dinosaurs without fear
- No source states the show's approach to scariness. (unconfirmed)
- The example above shows a dinosaur as a caring parent. (confirmed in one summary)
- Suggestion: show dinosaurs in routines (eating, nesting, caring for young), with humour. No predator chasing a child. Any danger falls on animals, not people.

## 4. Deadly 60 (Steve Backshall, CBBC)

Format
- Backshall and a camera crew travel the world to find 60 of the "most deadly" animals in a series. (confirmed)
- "Deadly" means formidable within its ecosystem, not dangerous to people. The focus is hunting and the animal's role in its ecosystem. (confirmed)
- Each episode features several animals. Each is followed in its habitat, with notable facts about its behaviour and ecosystem role. (confirmed)
- Shown on CBBC, and on BBC One, BBC Two and the CBBC Channel. (confirmed)
- Spin-offs: Deadly 60 Bites (5 to 10 minute segments) and Deadly Top 10s (a 30-minute countdown). (confirmed)
- Series numbering differs between sources. Check before quoting a series number. (conflict)
- "Adaptation" as a theme is not confirmed. The sources talk about hunting and ecosystem role.

Safety and respect
- The programme's own description does not cover safety, conservation or animal welfare. (confirmed absence)
- In interviews Backshall says: "deadly" does not mean dangerous to people; wild animals rarely harm people unless threatened; stay calm around animals because they sense fear; wildlife is fascinating, not frightening. (summary)
- The show's own animal-welfare rules are not in any source checked. (unconfirmed)

Phrases
- No catchphrases or sign-offs were found in any source. (confirmed absence)
- Do not attribute a tagline to the show. The writer should create their own.

Borrowable elements
- A numbered quest (the count of 60).
- Several creatures per episode, each with one "what makes it notable" fact.
- Tracked-in-habitat framing: where it lives, how it hunts, what it eats.
- A respectful tone: the animal is the hero, not a threat.

- https://www.howitworksdaily.com/interview-steve-backshall/ (summary)
- https://www.emmahigginbotham.com/recent-stuff/an-interview-with-steve-backshall (summary)

## 5. Ten-beat template: the Explorer Case

Use this template for every Explorer Case. It has two choice points, each with fold-back (branches that rejoin a shared page).

1. HQ opening. Finn and Zeb at base. One line about the mission. Fixed.
   - Borrowed from: the Octopod base (Octonauts) and the league headquarters (Creature Cases).
2. Call for help or clue. A call, message or clue. Fixed.
   - Borrowed from: the agents' call (Creature Cases) and the museum mishap (Andy).
3. Gear up and vehicle. Pick up gear and one vehicle. Fixed.
   - Borrowed from: the Gups, each based on a real animal (Octonauts), and the gadgets (Creature Cases).
4. Arrival. The team reaches the place. Fixed.
   - Borrowed from: the region opening (Creature Cases) and the habitat (Deadly 60).
5. First choice. Two ways in, for example over the water or through the trees. Branching.
   - Borrowed from: the fork in a choose-your-own-adventure book.
6. Meeting the creature or place. The same creature whichever route the reader took. Fixed, and the rejoin point.
   - Borrowed from: the Octonauts meeting, and Deadly 60 tracking.
7. Dilemma with no single right answer. Two or three picture options. Each leads to a short branch. Branching.
   - Borrowed from: the suspects and false leads (Creature Cases) and the mishap (Andy).
8. The real feature helps. A trait of the creature or place solves the problem. Fixed, with branch-specific lines only.
   - Borrowed from: the Octonauts rule that the real feature solves the problem, and Deadly 60 hunting facts.
9. Home and "case cracked". Back at base, the problem is solved. Fixed.
   - Borrowed from: the return to the present (Andy) and the case solved (Creature Cases).
10. Case File. Three real facts, each with a picture and a source. Fixed.
    - Borrowed from: the Fact File (Creature Cases), the Creature Report (Octonauts) and the field facts (Deadly 60).

Which beats suit a branching choice
- Beats 5 and 7 are the best choice points. They are clear, separate and work with picture options.
- Beats 3 and 4 should stay fixed. A choice before the creature appears can make the story feel unfocused.
- Beats 6, 8, 9 and 10 stay fixed so the rescue and the facts land for both children.

Structure (suggestion)
- Two choice points, two options each. Three options at most for a dilemma.
- Each branch runs one to two spreads, then rejoins at beat 6 or beat 8.
- About 24 to 32 pages in total.

Constraints (suggestion)
- No one in real danger. Animals may hunt, but no one is chased or hurt on the page. This matches Backshall's interviews and the gentle tone of Octonauts.
- One to three sentences per card.
- Each fact card has one number, a comparison a 3-year-old can picture, and a source.

## 6. Choose-your-own-adventure tips for ages 5 to 7

Evidence note: no children's publisher guide was found, so most of this section is suggestion.

Number of choices
- Classic choose-your-own-adventure books offer two or three options after a few pages. The originals were aimed at 7 to 14-year-olds. (confirmed)
- Picture-book readers: a choice every two or three spreads. Two options for pre-readers, three at most. (suggestion)
- One author-blog review of a younger choose-your-own-adventure book describes a choice on most or every other page. (summary; one report)

Branch length
- The sources do not set a length. (confirmed absence)
- Keep each branch to one or two spreads before it rejoins. (suggestion)

Fold-back structure
- The sources do not use the terms "fold-back" or "bottleneck". The Wikipedia entry says it does not describe whether paths merge. (confirmed)
- Paths rejoin at a shared page, so a choice changes a line or a picture, not the whole story. This keeps read-aloud smooth for both children. (suggestion)

Avoiding fail states
- Many classic gamebooks have one winning ending and many failure endings. Branching-plot novels often have several winning endings. (confirmed)
- Some choose-your-own-adventure books have trap endings reached by accident, with a reset to start again. (confirmed)
- For this age: no game-over endings. Every ending is warm or curious. A "try another way" page sends the reader back to the choice. (suggestion)
- Three to five endings for a short book. The early books had 7 to 44 endings, but they were longer. (confirmed range; size is a suggestion)

Making choices readable for 5 to 7-year-olds
- Show choices as pictures to point at, with one consistent symbol for "turn to". (suggestion)
- Each choice page must work with pictures alone, for Zeb. (suggestion)
- Test a draft by reading it aloud to Finn and Zeb and checking whether they can follow the choice. (suggestion)

Examples to look at
- You Choose (Kane Miller): the publisher page says the book is never the same twice. It shows no age range or format. A parent-blogger review lists You Choose Fairy Tales at 3 to 7. Check the listing before using it as a model. (publisher page confirmed; age from summary)
- Endlessly Ever After (Laurel Snyder and Dan Santat): a choose-your-path fairy tale picture book. (summary)
